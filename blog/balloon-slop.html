Balloon slop


After building a workflow and committing myself to spending an unconscionable amount of money on generating AI slop (see I'm joining the war on slop, on the side of the slop), I chose balloons for the next subject of my AI slop explorations, for several reasons:
  1. Aesthetic interest: hoping to get colorful, texturally-interesting content.
  2. Erotic appeal: while I don't count myself among them, I am aware that balloons are an object of some fetishization in the globophilic community also known as "looners," who find one or more aspects of the balloon erotic: latex surfaces and inflation (which have crossover appeal to other fetish communities), popping, and near-popping. I apologize in advance to the looners.
  3. Physics: balloons have specific ways of being and interacting with the world that are governed by laws of physics that I don't believe that AI video models have fully grasped or simulated, so I knew there would be some weirdness.
Using the prompt above, I generated hundreds of 10-second clips and sorted them into thematically similar buckets. The result is a series of 9 new videos for The Slop Factory Outlet: Inflations, Strange Inflations, Pops, Near Pops, Rubbing/Knotting, Mylars, Too Many Balloons, Balloon Animals, and Letting Go. They are each embedded here and ranked out of ten in pursuit of aesthetically interesting, erotically charged, physics-defying turbo slop.

Inflations

The inflation clips are grouped roughly by human involvement, color, and size, as well as grouped by gender of the humans involved. The few clips in this compilation are what I would call successful inflations: the puffing of cheeks or whirring of machines pretty much matches the rate at which the balloon expands. The settings are not wildly unrealistic. The physics almost work.
For aesthetic interest, I give them a 5 out of 10.
Erotic appeal: also a 5, I believe some people may find these clips sexually compelling.
Physics: 9 out of 10 (there are some glitches).
The only thing that I find truly strange and notable about the successful inflations are the people: Bob Odenkirk is here, shades of an Emma Watson type, two women who resemble Nicole Kidman and Courtney Cox (or perhaps Julia Roberts). When I started using this workflow for balloons I had kept the prompt stipulation of NO HUMANS from the original slop factory. However, balloons typically need a human or machine around to inflate them. Without them, you get Strange Inflations.

Strange Inflations

Balloons inflating themselves without any human influence, or in defiance of human manipulation are certifiably strange. They're alive, writhing, breathing themselves into existence. They are outgrowing their containers, budding and colonizing spaces. They are grotesque.
Aesthetically interesting: 10/10. Erotic appeal: either a -1 or a 10, these are freaky. Physics: 0.

Pops

These balloon pops are roughly ordered by popping method: first by puncturing, followed by overinflation, then by force (squeezing, crushing), then by accident (encounters with animals, barbeque grills, birthday candles, cigarettes, christmas trees, and cacti).
Aesthetic interest: 7 out of 10. I like the variety of situations where a balloon can be exploded.
Physics: 4 out of 10. Despite these being my best pops, they do not hold up to much scrutiny.
Erotic appeal: if you can get past the subpar physics, I believe these could be 6 out of 10 or higher. There must be something that someone likes here, or it wouldn't be here. I would put some money on popping due to overinflation (overtones of premature ejaculation), or popping by force. I'm sure I could specialize in shoes/feet or cars popping balloons based on how often those appeared in the generations.
You'll also notice in the popping clips the emergence of a singular location which I've dubbed The Toolshed. Many of the AI generated male actor personas (including poor Bob Odenkirk) often appear in this place defined by its walls lined with tools and low lighting. The Toolshed is clearly inside of a garage and set up as some kind of man cave or home workshop. Woman are never featured in The Toolshed: as you may have noticed in Inflations they are most often, sadly, still in the kitchen (thanks, AI). I can't imagine this is a specifically balloon-related generated location. My best guess is this is simply Where Men Do Things.

Near Pops

There are only a few of these, but I think they're gold: starting off strong with shoes and feet, thighs, and then hands and machines squeezing and threatening to pop their balloons. Aesthetic interest: low, maybe 3/10, on account of the scarcity of good near-pop clips. Erotic appeal: high, presumably, 6 or 7 out of 10. Physics: maybe 5/10. I really don't like the noises, and am not sure they match up with the movements. I realized after watching hundreds of these clips that I may no longer know what a real balloon sounds like.

Rubbing/Knotting

Here the lines between the balloons and the body blur quite a bit and it gets, frankly, gross. Viewer discretion is advised. I think some of these generations came out of the part of the prompt soliciting balloons rubbing on things or on each other in what I hoped would be a materially interesting static-cling-generating or a squeaky-latex way. What it produced was balloons as obvious surrogates for palpating breasts, a fixation on balloon knots, and balloons having a new kind of object-on-object knot sex. The last clips of flesh-colored balloons are by far the worst things I have generated (besides perhaps one pile of eyes prompted by just the word "shoggoth"). The last clip is quite clearly just puppetry of the penis.
Aesthetic interest: 10/10 if you're into body horror. Erotic appeal: god I hope not, but we don't kinkshame here. Physics: 0 out of 10.

Mylars

Mylar balloons, in contrast to what we've seen so far with their latex counterparts, are so normal. They are a working balloon, found in the most quotidian settings: the DMV, the grocery store, the hospital waiting room. They are here to deliver a clear message: Get Well, Congratulations, celebrate a milestone. Most of these clips come with pensive, soft piano music. The mylar balloon fits into daily life in a way that its gaudy latex cousin does not.
Aesthetic interest: 9 out of 10, each of these balloon clips seems to want to begin a poignant short film. Erotic appeal: 0. Physics: mostly 9/10, these balloons aren't doing anything weird. It is a bit unsettling to occasionally see the reflection of a camera person on the surface of the balloon, but again I applaud the realism.

Too Many Balloons

Often, AI is way overdoing it with the balloons. These clips have simply too many balloons. Impossible quantities of balloons spilling out of doorways, seas of balloons heaving as they cover the floors of airplane hangers and ceilings of ballrooms, balloons festooning improbable parade floats, and finally impossible quantities of balloons being released in stadiums acting like particle physics demos, accompanied by great fanfare. Warning to the viewer, this one also does contain some popping sounds.
Aesthetic interest: 8 out of 10, for the pure slop of it. This is balloons to the nth degree. Balloons as pure texture.
Erotic appeal: 0.
Physics: 0.

Balloon Animals

I was hoping with "new balloon animals" in the prompt I would get some innovations in the field of balloon animal artistry. The same way that AI is being used to design new viruses by brute forcing permutations of biological and molecular structures, I assumed new and exciting species of balloon animal would naturally emerge. I was mistaken, or should have tried harder. They were mostly crap. I limited this compilation to clips of balloon dogs and sorted them by color instead of species for this reason. Jeff Koons, eat your heart out. Aesthetic interest: 4/10. Erotic appeal: 0. Physics: 5/10. They do sometimes move of their own accord, but that could also be the wind.

Letting Go

This is a compilation of 10-second clips where a balloon is set free in the air and the camera's eye follows as it shrinks up into the sky, sorted by the color of the sky. Aesthetic interest: 3/10. Erotic appeal: 0. Physics: 2/10, a lot of these balloons seem to get free at approximately 100 miles per hour. A meditation on plastic pollution, helium conservation, and letting go.
If your reaction to all of this is "Thanks, I hate it," you're welcome! I hope you did not learn anything new about yourself today.
